# Look O: Wiki

Mock-up: `O-wiki.html` (preview picture: `O-wiki-preview.png`). Generated by `_gen-MNO\build.ps1` from the site's real themes, backgrounds and fonts.

## Rationale
An administrator's dashboard is closest to a living runbook: a set of pages that say what is true now, what to check first and what changed. A documentation layout is already the most familiar way to read that kind of thing, quickly, from top to bottom, with a table of contents to jump to a section. In Wiki the dashboard reads in sentences: "4 items need a decision from you today. APP-02 is down and has not replied for 14 minutes." Status lives inside the running text as small inline badges, and the one thing that needs checking first is a boxed note at the top of Systems. Pages are in a tree on the left, sections in a list on the right, so scanning is by heading, not by tile.

## Layout rules (what replaces rounded cards and accent rails)
- Three columns: page tree (252px), the article (up to 790px, left aligned), "On this page" list (224px). A plain 48px header bar holds the name, a "Find a page" box and "Last edited".
- The article is one bordered sheet. Inside it: breadcrumb, a title, a one-line meta ("Updated Tuesday 24 November, 22:51. Owner: Alex."), an intro sentence, then sections (H2 with a thin rule, with a "#" link on hover).
- Lists of decisions are checklist rows with a thin rule between them. Tables have a header row, a heavy rule under it and thin rules between rows; no zebra stripes, no boxes.
- Status is an inline badge (a small bordered word: up, down, degraded, overdue, due 28 Nov) in text. A badge always says the word; colour is secondary and tinted from the status colour with strong text on top.
- A callout is a full-border note with a soft status tint and a bold first sentence ("Check APP-02 first."). It has no left bar.
- Footer of every page: "Edit this page", "History (4 versions)", "Last edit 22:51 by Claude."

## Type
IBM Plex Sans 400, 500 and 600 throughout (15px on 1.65 line height, 13.5px on 1.5 compact; lines under 76 characters). IBM Plex Mono 400 for ids and codes only (`it_a1`). Titles are Plex Sans 600, not a decorative face: this look gets its character from structure and wording.

## Icon approach
None. Disclosure and current page are shown by weight and a quiet background; checkboxes are drawn; every state has its word. The only glyph is "#" for section links.

## Tone of voice (5 rewrites)
| Today | Wiki |
|---|---|
| Waiting on you | Decisions you owe |
| Since you last looked | What changed today |
| In progress and soaking | Work in progress |
| Services | Systems |
| Weather / This week | Next seven days |
Rules: runbook voice: facts first, then what to do; "Check APP-02 first." not "Heads up!"; a page says when it was updated and by whom; no exclamation marks, no chat tone.

## AI clichés avoided
Card grids and tiles, big numbers with gradient, accent rail on notes, all-caps eyebrow labels, icons beside every item, emoji, centred hero, serif-and-cream palette, purple gradient, hover-lift on cards.

## Backgrounds
The article and the header are solid; the tree and the contents list have a translucent page colour (84%) so a pattern shows faintly behind them without touching text.
- Suit: Plain, Dots, Grid, Blueprint, Contours, Ledger (ruled desk behind a document), Stripes (subtle).
- Clash: Aurora, Mesh, Glow, Spotlight, Beams, Rays at strong strength are visible through the translucent side columns and compete with the tree text; they still pass contrast on their own computed tint but look loud. Use subtle or medium.

## What it would change in the shared shell
- `data-look="wiki"` on `<html>`, `look` in themes.js (validated; cookie; profile sync).
- shell.css layer scoped to `[data-look="wiki"]` (about 120 lines: header bar, tree, article, badges, callout, tables, contents list). Tokens unchanged.
- shell.js groups the existing menu into tree groups (Today, Work, Systems) and renders them as the left tree; the existing jump search becomes the header "Find a page" box. The top bar's theme and Settings controls stay.
- Page content wraps in an `<article>`; a small script builds "On this page" from the page's H2s with a scroll-spy (IntersectionObserver).
- Overview needs a "document" variant: each widget renders as a section of the article. Ops board maps to the grouped tables shown in the mock-up. This look is the least invasive of the three because the sidebar menu already has the right shape.

## Sidebar setting
`full`: the tree with group headings and page names. `icons`: the tree collapses to a 56px rail showing only each page's first letter in a square, with the full tree opening over the article on click or focus. "On this page" stays; below 1100px it is hidden, and below 760px the tree becomes a wrapped list above the article.

## Contrast (36 combinations, tokens from themes.js, `_gen-MNO\contrast.ps1`)
No pair fails 4.5:1. Lowest ratios: text on article 11.81 (Steel dark), strong text on article 15.71, dim text on article and header 5.57 (Arctic light), text on the translucent side columns 10.76 (Stone light) with strong text 15.46 and dim text 5.04 (Arctic light), strong text on the alert, ok, warn and info badges and callout at least 12.13 (Steel dark; badge tints are alpha-blended over the article before measuring), strong text on code and badge chips 14.44 (Arctic dark). The accent token is never used as text.

## Effort estimate
6 to 7 Engineer days: shell layer and header 1.5, tree from the existing menu 1, article wrapper, contents list and scroll-spy 1, Overview and Ops document renderers 2, Settings Look choice and sync 0.5, 36-combination test and review 1.

## Open doubts
Long tables (Vulnerabilities) need horizontal scroll inside the article. "Last edited by Claude" is a placeholder: the real dashboard has no page edit history, so the footer would show the last data refresh, not an edit. The search box in the mock-up filters page names only; the real one would reuse the site's jump search.
